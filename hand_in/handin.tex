We build the binomial tree as follows. The starting point is the closing 
price of Apple on April 28, 2026, denoted $S_0$. At each period, the 
price either goes up (multiplied by $u$) or down (multiplied by $d$), 
where $\Delta t = T/n = 0.02$ years. Both factors are calibrated using 
the volatility $\sigma$ estimated from Apple's daily log-returns over 
2020--2026. The larger $\sigma$, the larger $u$ and the smaller $d$.

Starting from $S_0$, each node splits into two branches: an up-branch 
($S \times u$) and a down-branch ($S \times d$). After 25 periods, the 
tree contains $2^{25} \approx 33$ million possible paths. At each node, 
we store the cumulative sum $C_t = \sum_{j=0}^{t} S_j$, needed to 
compute the arithmetic average $\bar{S}_n = C_n/(n+1)$.

The distribution of $A_T$ is highly right-skewed: the median (4.49) is well below the mean 
(15.59). More than 25\% of terminal nodes have a payoff of zero, reflecting paths where 
$S_n < \bar{S}_n$, i.e. the terminal stock price falls below the arithmetic average and the 
option expires worthless. The maximum payoff of 321.58 corresponds to the most favourable 
path, where the stock price increases at every period.
\section{Payoff at Terminal Nodes}
\label{sec:payoff}
The distribution of $A_T$ is highly right-skewed: the median is zero, meaning more than half 
of all terminal paths yield a worthless option, i.e. $S_n < \bar{S}_n$. Notably, the standard 
deviation (24.71) exceeds the mean (15.71), reflecting the high dispersion of payoffs. Most 
paths expire worthless while a small number of highly favourable paths drive the mean upward. 
The maximum payoff of 321.58 corresponds to the path where the stock price increases at every 
period.
